Hear the fall.
Learn the technique.

In a backward breakfall the hips land first, the hands slap the mat, and the rounded back rolls down a split second later. It is over in about half a second, too fast to watch yourself do it. This trainer gives each landing its own sound, so you can hear whether the order and the timing were right.

Mat line

Not calibrated yet, so no sounds will play. Press Calibrate, lie flat on your back across the picture and stay still when the voice says so. The line is fitted through your hips and shoulders and placed just above your body. It follows a tilted camera.

On a computer, press C to calibrate and Esc to cancel. The head crossing the line always sounds the alarm. An elbow on the mat gives a soft caution.

Why sound, and why these sounds

Falls are the second leading cause of unintentional-injury death worldwide. In judo, ukemi (safe falling) trains the body to spread an impact over a fixed sequence of landings. The hard part for a teacher is that what matters is the order and timing of those landings. They happen within roughly 300 to 600 ms, and the person falling cannot see them.

"Auditory perception is particularly sensitive to temporal characteristics… perception of sound does not require the listener to be oriented in a particular direction."Kramer et al., 1999

Sound does not compete with what the eyes are doing during a fall, and hearing resolves onsets to within a few milliseconds. That is the case for sonification, defined as "the transformation of data relations into perceived relations in an acoustic signal."

From analogic to symbolic

Walker & Nees (Handbook Ch.2) describe a range of auditory displays. At one end are analogic sounds that resemble what they stand for. At the other are symbolic ones, where the link is arbitrary and has to be learned. Where a design sits on that range predicts how fast a listener understands it without being told, and that is the main question for a trainer people should be able to pick up and use.

Placement is ordinal. It follows the reasoning given for each design on the Sounds page and is not a measurement.

What follows for teaching

There is no single best sound. The seven designs sit at different points between analogic and symbolic, and each one tells the learner something different: which part landed, how hard it landed, or how the whole fall flowed. The choice depends on who is learning and on what the lesson is about.

Start a recording, let the participant practise in Watch or Train, fill in the questionnaire, then export. Everything is saved as CSV and JSON files on this device. There is no server and nothing is uploaded.

Recording

Logs every landing (body part, sound mode, level, time, intensity), completed sequences and head errors. With the camera running it also stores all 33 pose landmarks.

Use an anonymous code, never a name.

Export

JSON holds everything, including the pose data. CSV gives one table of events and one row of questionnaire answers, ready for R, SPSS or Python.
